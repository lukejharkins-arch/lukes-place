# THE OTHER SIDE OF THE GLASS | origin film

**Format:** 90-second hero film, cut down to 60 / 30 / 15 and vertical social versions.
**Characters:** entirely fictional. No real or identifiable athlete, team, school, logo or uniform design appears. Wardrobe is original ON COURT ONLY design in neutral team-like colourways. The point is distance, not waste: the player is busy, not careless.

## Screenplay (90 s)

**1. EXT. SUBURBAN OUTDOOR COURT, LATE AFTERNOON (0:00-0:10)**
A chain-link fence. Kids, around ten years old, play on a cracked court. SOUND: a ball on concrete, cicadas.
Through a window across the road we see MAX (16), a gifted player, open a box: a college recruiting pack. Warmup jacket, shooting shirt, shoes.

**2. INT. MAX'S BEDROOM (0:10-0:18)**
Max tries the jacket, grins, then his phone buzzes: training. He folds the gear neatly into a wardrobe and runs out.
Outside, the kids press against the fence and glass, eyes on that jacket on the chair. One of them, TESS (10), mouths "one day".

**3. MONTAGE: TIME (0:18-0:45)**
- Max in a college dorm; a stack of team-issued boxes, warmups on hangers. He studies film, exhausted, happy.
- Tess at 15, wearing a homemade "warmup" from a market stall, practising alone under a streetlight.
- Max in a pro tunnel; an equipment manager hands him fresh warmups. He nods thanks. SOUND: the snap of tearaways coming off, slowed and huge.
- Tess at 22, watching a game at 4 a.m. Australian time on a laptop; the camera holds on the warmups on screen.
The glass motif repeats: shop windows, TV screens, a display case. Always a pane between Tess and the gear.

**4. INT. EQUIPMENT ROOM, END OF SEASON (0:45-0:55)**
A staff member logs surplus apparel onto a clipboard marked RELEASE. A box is sealed with a provenance sheet. (Shows a legitimate release, not a player giving gear away.)

**5. INT. ON COURT ONLY STUDIO, AUSTRALIA (0:55-1:10)**
Hands in gloves photograph tags, check snaps, match a size label, file the paperwork. A screen shows the classification: TEAM-ISSUED · documented. A courier label: MELBOURNE.

**6. INT. TESS'S APARTMENT (1:10-1:22)**
Tess (now 28) opens the box. The warmup pants. She runs a thumb down the snaps, then pulls one: SNAP. She laughs. On the wall behind her is the polaroid of the outdoor court.

**7. EXT. THE SAME OUTDOOR COURT (1:22-1:27)**
Tess in the pants, shooting with today's ten-year-olds. No fence between them in the frame.

**END CARD (1:27-1:30)**
ON COURT ONLY
"Where basketball love meets authentic on-court wear."

## Production outline
- **Crew:** director, DOP, gaffer, sound recordist, production designer (original wardrobe), editor and colourist. Two shoot days in Melbourne or Sydney: day 1 interiors (bedroom, dorm, studio, apartment), day 2 exteriors (court, tunnel built from a stadium corridor or a hired venue).
- **Cast:** four ages of Tess (10, 15, 22, 28) and Max (16, 19, 25). Child performers under state working-with-children rules, with chaperones and permits.
- **Rights:** original score; the SNAP is recorded foley of our own garments; all wardrobe original; locations released; no broadcast footage; no logos. Talent releases for every performer covering paid social and web.
- **Budget guide (ESTIMATE, not approved):** lean crew A$12k-A$25k; phone-shot micro version A$1.5k-A$3k. Separate approval needed; not part of the inventory budget.

## Visual direction
Two looks matching the brand: past scenes are warm, grainy, 4:3-feeling (ON COURT OG); present scenes are cool, crisp and wide (CURRENT COURT). The glass is the visual through-line; it disappears only in frame 12.

## Social adaptations
- **15 s:** frames 6, 11, end card. Hook: "Remember the sound?"
- **30 s:** 3, 6, 8, 9, 11, 12. "The gear you saw. The gear you couldn't get."
- **60 s:** full story without the dorm and 4 a.m. beats.
- **Vertical 9:16:** re-framed for Reels, TikTok and Shorts; captions burned in; first second is the SNAP.
- **Stills:** frame 3 (kids at the glass) and frame 12 (no fence) as a diptych for the launch post.
