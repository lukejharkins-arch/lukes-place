## 1. Bottom line

1. **Recommended leader: C1, an AML/CTF Tranche 2 "compliance in practice" SaaS for small real estate agencies and buyer's agents, extending later to accountants and conveyancers.** It has the strongest mix of legally mandated, recurring demand and high gross margin, and it can be mostly self-serve. Base score **67.5**. Conservative score **56.5**. Confidence **medium**.
2. **Shortlist (recommended): C1 AML/CTF ops SaaS (67.5), C2 AI voice receptionist for allied health clinics (62.0), C4 NDIS and aged care audit-ready compliance subscription (61.0), C5 Support at Home client-matching lead-gen (60.0).**
3. **The leader does not change under conservative assumptions.** The gap over C2 narrows from 5.5 points to 5.0, which sits right on the brief's tie-break line. I recommend not applying the story tie-break. C1's lead comes from economics (margin, churn, automation), and the charter says better economics beats a better story. Harbour should make that call explicitly (Q11).
4. **Honest headline.** No candidate reaches $500k by 30 Nov 2028 under conservative assumptions. On my arithmetic, the best conservative outcome is roughly $250k to $300k. The base case reaches $500k only if acquisition from zero works at a CAC near $350 and setup is fully self-serve. The first paid test should test exactly that.

## 2. Method and evidence quality

- **Searches:** 29 web searches across all model types and the named Australian regulatory niches.
- **Page fetches:** blocked. Direct page fetches failed for austrac.gov.au, ailo.io, fundingcentre.com.au and realestatebusiness.com.au because the session's network allowlist blocks them. Prices therefore come from search-engine extracts of vendor pages, Capterra and GetApp listings, and regulator PDFs. **Every price must be re-checked on the live page before the 9 Dec pack.** Harbour: allow those hosts before the deep-dive phase.
- **Evidence ladder applied:**
  - Paid commitments and real spend: regulator mandates with penalties; named vendors with revenue or customer counts; per-placement fees.
  - Behaviour: marketplace listings and competitor density.
  - Survey intent: PEXA and Visibl surveys, weighted lower. Visibl is a vendor.
- **A market signal in its own right [O S72]:** every AU SMB niche I searched returned several AI-built one-page sites selling the same offer (tradie receptionists, NDIS packs, AML kits). Copying is now cheap and SEO is crowded. This lowers the score of any option that relies on SEO, or on a document whose only value is that it was generated by AI. It raises the score of options with workflow lock-in, recurring obligations, or a distribution channel that clones cannot easily buy.
- **Baseline arithmetic used for every revenue path.**
  - First paid revenue is assumed from March 2027, giving 21 billing months to 30 Nov 2028 [A].
  - Customers ramp linearly from 0 to N, so cumulative customer-months = N x (1+2+...+21)/21 = **11N**.
  - Cumulative revenue = 11 x N x monthly price, plus any setup fees. Churn is added separately as extra gross adds.
  - Founder hours: 5 a week x about 106 weeks = **about 520 hours**. $500,000 / 520 = **about $960 a founder hour, call it $1,000**.

**C1. AML/CTF Tranche 2 "compliance in practice" SaaS**
- **Buyer:** principal or licensee of a small real estate or buyer's agency. Later: small accounting and conveyancing practices.
- **Pain already paid for:**
  - A legal mandate with penalties. Obligations began 1 Jul 2026 [O S2].
  - About 80,000 new reporting entities [O S1].
  - Only about 45% had enrolled by mid-September 2026 [O S11]. AUSTRAC has started sending investigative notices [O S12].
  - The ongoing work is customer due diligence on every deal, record keeping, staff training, risk assessment updates and an annual compliance report for the period 1 Jul 2026 to 30 Jun 2027 [O S15].
- **Spend and benchmarks:**
  - AML Simple $79 to $250 a month; duely from $59; IntelliCompli $130 to $299; ClearAML $299; ID checks $5 to $8 [O S3 to S6].
  - AML Partners reports more than 300 clients in four months and $1m to $2m in revenue [O S9, S10, self-reported].
- **Competitors:** the firms above, plus First AML (REIWA preferred provider) [O S17], easyAML [O S18], and AUSTRAC's free starter kit for small agencies [O S7].
- **Reach from zero:**
  - Agency emails are published. Cold email may be lawful under the Spam Act's inferred-consent rule [A, legal check needed, Q1].
  - Google Ads on high-intent "AML real estate" searches.
  - Content timed to AUSTRAC enforcement news.
  - A later reseller channel through franchise groups or PEXA-adjacent tools [A].
- **Revenue path (base):**
  - $149 a month + $12 a month ID-check margin (4 checks x $3) = $161 a month, plus a $199 setup fee [A].
  - Revenue = 11 x 161 x N + 199 x N = 1,970N. **N = 500,000 / 1,970 = 254 customers by Nov 2028.**
  - With 1.5% monthly churn, about 280 gross adds, or about 13 a month.
  - 254 is 0.3% of 80,000 entities [O S1].
- **Founder hours:** build 150, acquisition automation 50, exceptions 0.5 hours a customer a year (about 130), admin 100, test and iterate 60, giving **about 490 hours** [A]. $500k / 490 = **about $1,020 an hour**. Passes, narrowly.
- **Automation:** high.
  - AI turns the AUSTRAC kit into a tailored program from an intake form.
  - CDD checklists per deal, and ID checks through a licensed verification API.
  - Training modules, a register, and annual-report prep.
  - The human exception is regulatory interpretation questions.
- **Validation within $500:** landing page plus self-serve checkout, Spam-Act-compliant cold email and $150 of Google Ads. Full design in section 7.
- **Story (7/10):** "Two part-timers and an AI take on a new law that 55% of businesses still have not touched." There is real deadline drama. It is less visual than a voice agent.

**C2. AI voice receptionist for allied health clinics**
- **Buyer:** owner or practice manager of a physio, chiro, psychology or podiatry clinic.
- **Pain already paid for:** missed calls become lost bookings. Reception wages are the alternative spend.
- **Spend and benchmarks:**
  - Frontli $329 a month [O S27, unverified].
  - Aeva claims 2,100+ practitioners [O S26, V].
  - Generic AU receptionists cost $149 to $300 a month [O S19].
  - Voice cost to us: about US$0.07 to US$0.31 a minute [O S28, S29].
- **Competitors:** at least seven AI receptionists already listed in Cliniko's connected-apps directory [O S25].
- **Reach from zero:** a Cliniko or Halaxy directory listing (terms unknown, Q7), Google Ads, cold email to clinics, and calling into a live demo line.
- **Revenue path (base):**
  - $249 a month [A]. **N = 500,000 / (11 x 249) = 183 customers.**
  - With 3% monthly churn, about 245 gross adds.
  - Gross margin about 60%: 400 minutes x about A$0.26 = about A$105 a month in usage [A].
- **Founder hours:** at risk.
  - Onboarding exceptions at 0.5 hours a customer = 120 hours.
  - Live-call failure handling at 0.15 hours a customer a month = about 290 hours.
  - Plus build. The total exceeds 520 unless onboarding is fully self-serve and support stays under 5 minutes a customer a month [A].
- **Automation:** medium. The agent runs itself, but failures are live and customer-facing. Calls involve health information under the Privacy Act 1988.
- **Validation within $500:** a demo line on a pay-as-you-go voice platform (about $30), $150 of ads, and a 14-day trial converting to paid.
- **Story (8/10):** audio-native. AI call recordings (with consent) suit an audio-led documentary well.

- **Risks:** legality and disclosure of referral fees under the new Act are unconfirmed (Q4). Reputational risk is high.
- **Story:** 7/10.
