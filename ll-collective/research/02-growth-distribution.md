## 1. Bottom line

1. **Zero-audience companies get their first 10 customers through direct outreach or a marketplace where buyers already search.** Content, SEO and build-in-public do not do it. Every credible account of a cold start says the first customers came from one-to-one contact or marketplace intent, not followers (S29, S30, S68, S69) **[O, anecdotal but consistent]**.
2. **The $500 cap is not the binding constraint for B2B. Founder minutes are.** At observed cold email meeting rates (0.34% median to 0.87% top quartile, S15) and an assumed 20% close rate, 10 customers need 50 meetings. At 45 minutes each, that is about 37 hours, or 7.5 weeks of the whole founder budget. **Any B2B model must sell asynchronously** (video walkthrough, sample output, checkout link) or it fails the leverage screen.
3. **Paid ads at $500 buy a message test, not a customer base.** Meta is the only platform where $150 to $250 plausibly yields enough leads to learn from (about 3 to 15 leads at AUD $15 to $50 CPL, S56, S57). On LinkedIn ads, $500 buys about one lead (median US$376 CPL, S27).
4. **SEO and AI search are a 2027/28 asset, not a 90-day channel.** New domains typically take 6 to 12 months to rank (S45, S46). AI Overviews cut organic clicks by 38% to 61% on the queries where they appear (S47, S48). Start the compounding assets now (free tool, data pages) but plan zero revenue from them before about Q3 2027 **[F]**.
5. **The documentary is a trust asset and a recruiting asset, not an acquisition channel, until about 10,000 relevant followers** **[A]**. Build it on YouTube (Australian reach 77.7%, S60) and TikTok (best reach for accounts under 2,000 followers, S62). Measure it by clicks to the offer, not views.
6. **Spam Act risk is real but manageable.** It is real because ACMA has issued at least five penalties of $650k or more since March 2026 (S5 to S8). It is manageable because one-to-one, role-relevant email to conspicuously published business addresses can be lawful under Schedule 2 clause 4, provided the sender keeps evidence, identifies itself and honours unsubscribes within 5 working days (S1 to S4).

## 3. Channel detail

### 3.1 Cold email to Australian businesses

**What the law allows (Spam Act 2003 and ACMA guidance). VERIFY all points.**

- **Scope.** The Act covers commercial electronic messages with an Australian link, both business-to-business and business-to-consumer (S4) **[O]**. B2B is not exempt.
- **Consent (s 16).** You need express or inferred consent. Schedule 2 clause 4 allows consent to be inferred from conspicuous publication when all of these hold (S2, S3) **[O, paraphrased]**:
  - The address has been conspicuously published, for example on the business's own website or in a directory.
  - It is reasonable to assume it was published with the account holder's agreement.
  - The publication carries no statement that the holder does not want unsolicited commercial messages.
  - The message is relevant to the business, functions or duties of the published role. Example: a bookkeeping automation offer to a published "accounts@" or practice-manager address is relevant. A wine offer to the same address is not (S3, Dynamic Business example).
- **Limits.** ACMA treats inferred consent as "not as reliable" as express consent. It weighs the relationship, the relevance and the recipient's reasonable expectations (S1, S11) **[O]**. ACMA rejected inferred consent in SpinTel (Nov 2026), where a past customer relationship was not enough (S5) **[O]**. The burden of proof sits with the sender (S1) **[O]**.
- **Identification (s 17).** Each message must clearly identify the authorising business and include accurate contact details, valid for at least 30 days after sending (S2) **[O]**. Latitude paid $3.96m partly for inaccurate sender information (S7) **[O, Apr 2026]**.
- **Unsubscribe (s 18).** Every message needs a functional, free unsubscribe, actioned within 5 working days (S1, S6) **[O]**. Lululemon paid $702,900 for 370,289 messages without one (S6, Mar 2026). An unsubscribe from one channel must be honoured on that channel (TAB, $2.7m, S8, Jul 2026) **[O]**.
- **Address harvesting (Part 3).** The Act prohibits supplying, acquiring or using address-harvesting software, or a list produced by it, in connection with sending messages that breach s 16 (S2) **[O, paraphrased, VERIFY]**. Practical reading **[A, get advice]**:
  - Do not build or run an agent that crawls the web to collect email addresses.
  - If a licensed database is used, the defence is still that each message meets clause 4. Keep, per address, the URL where it is published, the date, a screenshot and the role.
- **Penalties.** The court maximum was quoted at $626,000 a day with no prior record and $3.13m a day with one (S9, Oct 2024). It has since been indexed upward, with about $660,000 quoted in 2025 (S10) **[O, VERIFY current]**. ACMA infringement notices in 2026 ran from $653k to $3.96m (S5 to S8) **[O]**.
- **Privacy Act.** The small business exemption (turnover under $3m) remains for now (S12, Sep 2026) **[O]**. It is lost if the business trades in personal information. Automated decision-making transparency starts 10 Dec 2026 for APP entities (S13) **[O]**. Tranche 2 reforms are at exposure draft and could narrow the exemption in 2027/28 **[F]**. Design as if fully covered.

**Compliance rules for LL Collective (proposed) [A]**

- Send one-to-one, low-volume email to role addresses or named role-holders whose address is conspicuously published on their own business website.
- Keep the offer tightly tied to the role.
- Identify LL Collective with its ABN and a real address.
- Include a one-click unsubscribe and an automatic suppression list.
- Honour opt-outs on the same day (the law allows 5 working days).
- Never use harvesting tools.
- Log the evidence for every address.
- Do not send to addresses listed only on aggregator sites unless counsel agrees.

**Deliverability realities in 2026**

- **Sender rules.** Gmail, Yahoo and Microsoft (Microsoft consumer mailboxes from 5 May 2025) require SPF, DKIM and DMARC (S18) **[O]**. The spam complaint ceiling is 0.3%, and Google's target is under 0.1%. Google moved to hard rejections from Nov 2025 (S19) **[O]**. The 5,000 a day bulk threshold will not apply at our volumes, but authentication still matters.
- **Inbox placement.** Validity's 2026 data puts Gmail at 87.2% and Outlook at 75.6% (S20, Jul 2026) **[O, via vendor]**. Many Australian SMBs run Microsoft 365, so expect worse placement there **[A]**.
- **Practitioner norm [A].**
  - Use separate secondary domains, never the main brand domain.
  - Warm up for 2 to 3 weeks.
  - Send 20 to 30 new emails per mailbox per day.
  - Use plain text, with no links or images in the first email.

**Funnel arithmetic for a 90-day test [A unless marked]**

- **Sending volume.** 3 mailboxes × 25 emails a day × 21 sending days = 1,575 new contacts a month. About 6 weeks of sending fit after warm-up, giving about 2,300 contacts.
- **Replies.** At 2.1% to 3.43% **[O]**, that is 48 to 79 replies.
- **Meetings.** At 0.34% to 0.87% **[O]**, that is 8 to 20 meetings. At an assumed 20% close rate, that is **2 to 4 customers in the first 90 days**.
- **Reaching 10 customers.** That needs 50 meetings, or about 5,700 to 14,700 contacts. **Reaching 100 customers** needs about 57,000 to 147,000 contacts. That exceeds what is lawfully and practically sourceable in a narrow Australian vertical, so cold email gets us to 10, and referral, partners and marketplaces must carry us to 100.
- **Founder time with calls.** About 45 minutes per meeting, so 50 meetings is about 37.5 hours, which breaks the budget.
- **Founder time with async selling.** The CTA is "reply YES and we'll send your personalised sample", with an AI-made sample and a checkout link. Founder time falls to about 5 to 10 minutes per positive reply for review **[A]**.
- **Automation.** Sending, sequencing, reply classification, sample generation and CRM logging are all automatable. Human review of positive replies stays.
- **Risks.**
  - Legal (above).
  - Domain or mailbox suspension by Google or Microsoft if complaints rise. Mitigate with secondary domains.
  - Tool vendor dependence.
  - Reputation, which matters more because we are filming this.

### 3.2 LinkedIn outreach and content

- **Volume ceiling.** About 100 connection requests per rolling 7 days, and lower for new profiles. This is a community estimate; LinkedIn does not publish it (S24) **[O, low grade]**.
- **Automation is prohibited.** LinkedIn bans third-party software that scrapes or automates activity, and warns of restriction or shutdown (S22, S23) **[O, A grade]**. Treat LinkedIn as manual only. That makes it founder-minute heavy, at 15 to 20 minutes per real conversation **[A]**.
- **Conversion math [A].**
  - About 100 requests a week at 25% to 35% acceptance gives 25 to 35 new connections.
  - At 10% to 15% conversation rate, that is about 3 to 5 conversations a week.
  - The cost is about 60 to 90 founder minutes a week.
  - Over 13 weeks, that is about 40 to 60 conversations and perhaps 2 to 5 customers for a B2B service.
- **Organic reach.** Median post impressions fell 47%, from 1,211 to 636, between Jun 2024 and May 2025 across 621,000 posts (S25) **[O]**. The decline levelled off in Q1 2026, down 2% quarter on quarter (S26) **[O]**. Personal profiles drew 2.75 times the impressions of company pages (Refine Labs, via S26) **[O]**. Post from founder profiles, not a company page.
- **Australian reach.** About 18m registered members, which is not the same as active users (S61) **[O]**.
- **Ads.** CPL is a spend-weighted US$202 and a median US$376 (S27, 2025 data) **[O]**. That is about AUD $310 to $580 per lead, so it is ruled out for the first 90 days.
- **Role.** LinkedIn is a credibility layer: prospects who get our cold email check it. It is also a home for documentary clips aimed at B2B buyers. It is a secondary manual channel for a B2B service only.

### 3.4 SEO, programmatic SEO and AI search

- **Time to traffic.**
  - Semrush tracked 28,000 new sites; only 4.2% held a top-10 ranking throughout 13 months (S45) **[O]**.
  - Google has said it can take up to a year to work out where a new site ranks (S46) **[O]**.
  - Expect the first meaningful organic leads 6 to 12 months after launch, around Jun to Dec 2027 if started in Dec 2026 **[F]**.
- **AI Overviews.**
  - Organic CTR on AIO queries fell from 1.76% to 0.61% (Seer, S47) **[O]**.
  - A randomised field study found a 38% cut in clicks (S48, 2026) **[O]**.
  - Informational content is worth less. Commercial-intent and tool pages hold up better **[A]**.
- **AI search referrals.**
  - In a 973-site ecommerce study, ChatGPT visitors converted at 1.81% against 1.39% for non-branded organic, but at lower revenue per session. Google organic still drove 27 times the conversions of all LLMs combined (S49) **[O]**.
  - Ahrefs reports AI search was 0.5% of its visitors but 12.1% of its signups (S50) **[O, one company]**.
  - Being cited matters more than ranking. Third-party listings, reviews, marketplace pages and specific factual pages are what LLMs cite **[A]**.
- **Programmatic SEO.**
  - Scaled content abuse was a stated enforcement focus in the March 2026 update (S51) **[O, secondary]**, and spam policies now formally cover AI Overviews and AI Mode (S52) **[O]**.
  - Template pages are viable only when each page carries unique, useful data, for example an AI-calculated benchmark for each Australian industry or postcode from licensed or open data. Thin AI pages are a liability **[A]**.
- **Cost:** about $20 to $60 for a domain and hosting in 90 days **[A]**.
- **Founder minutes per lead:** near zero after setup.
- **Automation:** high (an AI drafts, a human approves).
- **Risks:** algorithm updates, AI answer engines absorbing clicks, and penalties for scaled pages.
- **Role:** start one free tool and a small set of data pages in Dec 2026. Treat them as a 2027/28 compounding asset.

### 3.6 Build in public and short-form video

**Evidence that founder-journey content converts**

- Successes are real but come from years of compounding. One example is Marc Lou's self-reported US$1.03m in 2025 with about 300k X followers (S70) **[O, self-reported]**.
- For zero-audience founders, the documented pattern is reach without buyers. Examples: "8 weeks of building in public got me real reach and basically zero signups" (S68), and "your audience is other founders, not your market" (S69) **[O, anecdotal]**.
- I found no audited follower-to-customer conversion rate. Treat build-in-public as **zero direct lift** in the base model, consistent with the brief's "model zero viral lift" screen **[A]**.

**Platform reach for small accounts**

- Across 375,462 accounts, TikTok leads views for accounts under 2,000 followers and gets nearly twice the views per video of YouTube Shorts (S62, 2026) **[O]**.
- Engagement rates in 2026 are TikTok 2.60%, Reels 0.45% and Shorts 0.30% (S63) **[O]**.
- Australian reach: YouTube 21.0m people (77.7%) and Facebook 17.7m (S60) **[O]**. TikTok has about 10.9m adult users (S61) **[O]**.
- The under-16 social media ban started 10 Dec 2025, so expect lower teen reach (S61) **[O]**. This matters only for B2C.

**AI-voice and faceless formats specifically**

- **YouTube.** From 15 Jul 2025, "inauthentic" content (mass-produced, templated, repetitive) cannot be monetised. AI use and AI voiceover are allowed when the content is original and adds value (S64) **[O]**. The risk is to monetisation and reach, not to the account, unless the content is spam.
- **TikTok.** Realistic AI-generated content must be labelled. Per TikTok's help guidance the label itself does not cut distribution. TikTok began testing detection of AI-spam accounts on 10 Jul 2026 (S66) **[O, C grade]**.
- **Ads.** AI-generated ad creative needs disclosure labels from Jul 2026 (S65) **[O, C grade]**.
- **Implication [A].** A faceless format with an AI narrator works only if every episode carries something only we have: real numbers, real decisions, real failures and real customer outcomes. The documentary premise supplies exactly this. Templated "10 AI tools" clips would be classed as low-value AI content.
- **Faceless digital-product revenue claims.** Figures such as "US$5k to $15k a month" come from course sellers and are unverified (search results reviewed 18 Nov 2026) **[O, D grade]**.

**X (Twitter).** X says it removed the link penalty (Jul 2026), but independent data is mixed (S67) **[O]**. Its Australian SMB buyer presence is low **[A]**. Use it only as a cross-post.

**Product Hunt.** A top-3 finish brings 5,000 to 15,000 visitors and a top-10 finish 1,000 to 3,000. B2B signups from it are weak (S71) **[O, C grade]**. It is worth one launch for backlinks and documentary footage, not as a customer channel.

- **Cost:** $0 to $60 for 90 days (AI voice and editing tools) **[A]**.
- **Founder minutes:** 60 to 90 a week for approving scripts and recording real moments **[A]**.
- **Automation:** high for editing, captioning, cross-posting and scheduling.
- **Time to first customer:** 3 to 9 months **[A]**.
- **Risks:** platform policy on AI content, account bans for spam patterns, reputational risk if the story goes badly (also good footage), and screen time stolen from selling.

### 3.7 Referral and sharing loops

**What customers of AI tools actually share [O and A]**

- **Outputs that make the sharer look good.** AI headshots are one example. HeadshotPro reports US$10m+ revenue and 17m headshots (S76, self-reported). It credits affiliates (career coaches) with more than US$50k a month, over 15% of revenue (S75) **[O]**. Another example is stylised images: ChatGPT's Ghibli-style images drove 1m signups in an hour on 31 Mar 2025 (S74) **[O, company claim]**.
- **Artefacts the recipient must open, carrying a badge.** Examples are "Made with Typeform", "Recorded with Loom" and "Edit with Lovable". Lovable makes badge removal a paid feature (S72) **[O]**, and the mechanism is documented in S73 **[O, C grade]**. The loop works because recipients see the product in use.
- **Scores, benchmarks and reports with the customer's name on them [A].** Examples are "your business scored 72/100 on automation readiness, top 30% of Melbourne cafés". People forward these to a partner, accountant or staff, and the footer carries our brand.
- **Affiliate and referral payouts.** The median SaaS affiliate commission is 20% recurring, and 71% of programs pay recurring (S77) **[O, C grade]**. Referral shares of new SaaS customers are quoted at 10% to 40% (S78) **[O, D grade]**.

**Design implication for LL Collective [A]**

- Whatever the business is, the product should emit a shareable artefact: a report, score, badge, certificate or client-facing document.
- Put a "made with" footer on the free tier, and make removing it a paid feature.
- Pay 20% recurring to referrers.
- Cost to build: $0 cash, roughly 2 to 6 hours of build time **[A]**.
- **Time to effect:** zero before the first 10 customers. It is the main lever from 10 to 100 **[F]**.
- **Risks:** low. Badges must not mislead, and incentivised reviews breach platform rules.

### 3.8 Partnerships and resellers

- **Accountants and bookkeepers.** Xero's program has about 250,000 accountants and bookkeepers globally (S79) **[O]**, and Xero curates apps "most recommended by advisors" (S35) **[O]**. Advisors influence which tools SMBs adopt. A 20% recurring referral fee plus a co-branded client report is the standard offer **[A]**.
- **MSPs.** 48% of 1,000+ MSPs rank AI and automation as their clients' top need for 2026, but only 13% earn meaningful revenue from it (Kaseya, S80) **[O]**. MSPs make margin from services that cut their cost-to-serve, not from reselling licences (S81) **[O]**. Australian MSPs face a skills shortage that is pushing them to automation (S82, Jan 2026) **[O]**. A white-label AI automation that an MSP resells is a credible 2027 channel **[F]**.
- **Industry associations.** Member-benefit listings and newsletter features cost little cash but are slow, at 2 to 6 months, and usually want a track record **[A]**.
- **Cost to test:** $0 to $50.
- **Founder minutes:** 60 to 120 per partner signed (calls and onboarding) **[A]**. After that, leads arrive at near zero founder minutes.
- **Automation:** medium (partner portal, tracked links and auto-payouts).
- **Time to first customer:** 2 to 6 months **[A]**.
- **Partner outreach:** cold email to partners is subject to the same Spam Act rules.
- **Risks:** partner churn, channel conflict, and partners wanting exclusivity.
- **Recommendation:** start 3 to 5 partner conversations once there are 3 or more paying customers and one case study, not before.

## 4. Ranked recommendation: top three cold-start channels by model type

Ranking criteria: probability of 10 paying customers by 15 Feb 2027 within $500 and 5 hours a week, then scalability to 100.

### B2B SaaS

1. **Compliant cold email with an async trial CTA.** Email Australian businesses at role-relevant published addresses with a personalised sample output and a self-serve trial, without calls.
   - At an assumed 0.5% trial-start rate and 20% trial-to-paid, 2,300 contacts gives about 11 trials and 2 to 3 customers in 90 days **[A]**.
   - Cost about AUD $245.
2. **Ecosystem marketplace listing** (Shopify if the buyer is a merchant, Xero or HubSpot if SMB finance or ops), seeded with the cold email cohort to get the first 5 to 10 reviews.
   - Economics are best on Shopify (0% to US$1m, S28).
   - First organic installs arrive 1 to 3 months after reviews **[A]**.
3. **Free tool plus shareable-output loop** (badge or report), which also feeds SEO and AI-search citations. It is slow but compounds into 2027/28 **[F]**.

### B2B done-for-you AI service

1. **Compliant cold email in one narrow vertical, sold asynchronously.**
   - Send a free AI-made mini-audit or sample deliverable, then a fixed-price offer with a checkout link.
   - At observed meeting-equivalent rates of 0.34% to 0.87% (S15), 2,300 contacts gives 8 to 20 serious prospects and 2 to 4 customers **[A]**.
   - This model fails the leverage screen if every sale needs a call.
2. **Referral partners (bookkeepers, accountants, MSPs)** on 20% recurring. Start after the first 3 customers. This is the main route from 10 to 100 for a service **[F]**.
3. **Manual LinkedIn conversations plus founder-profile content.** Capacity is about 3 to 5 conversations a week for 60 to 90 minutes. It adds credibility to the cold emails and doubles as documentary B2B distribution.

### B2C digital product

1. **Short-form video with AI voice, honest numbers and real decisions.** Post on TikTok first, then cross-post to Reels and Shorts. Production and documentary share one pipeline. It is the only channel with near-zero founder minutes per lead, but expect 3 to 9 months to traction **[A]**.
2. **A marketplace with built-in search.** Use Etsy for templates and printables (fees S43; AI disclosure S44), or Gumroad for direct sales (10%, S42). The first sale is plausible within 1 to 6 weeks **[A]**.
3. **A shareable-output loop, plus a single Meta ads test** of AUD $100 to $150 to compare offers. At AUD $15 to $50 CPL that buys 3 to 10 leads (S56, S57).

**Cross-model note.** If the Research Lead's leader is B2B, cold email is the critical-path channel. The Spam Act legal check should be the first spend request. If the leader is B2C, the documentary content engine is the critical path.

## 7. Draft plan: social channels for the documentary

**Purpose.** Tell the true story of an AI-run company trying to reach $500k, build trust with buyers who check us out, and create a long-lived asset. Acquisition from it is a bonus, at zero lift in the base model.

| Channel | Role | Format | Cadence [A] | Why (evidence) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **YouTube (hub)** | Home of the documentary; searchable, long-lived | Monthly 10 to 20 minute episode; 3 to 5 Shorts cut from each | 1 episode a month, 3 Shorts a week | Highest Australian reach, 21.0m (77.7%, S60). Long-form is the documentary's natural home. Avoid templated repetition (S64) |
| **TikTok (discovery)** | Fastest reach for a new account | 30 to 60 second clips: one decision, one number, one lesson | 4 to 5 a week, same clips as Shorts and Reels | Best views for accounts under 2,000 followers (S62); highest engagement (S63). Label realistic AI content (S66) |
| **LinkedIn (founder profiles)** | B2B credibility and buyer trust | Text post plus clip from each episode; monthly numbers update | 2 a week | Personal profiles beat company pages 2.75x (S26); B2B buyers check here |
| **Instagram Reels** | Cross-post only | Same clips | Auto cross-post | Low marginal cost; Reels engagement 0.45% (S63) |
| **X** | Cross-post and build-in-public community | Numbers threads, clips | Auto cross-post | Indie and AI builder audience; Australian SMB buyers are thin there **[A]** |
| **Email newsletter (owned)** | The only channel we own | Monthly "what happened, what we spent, what we earned" | Monthly | Protects against platform dependence |

**Production pipeline (automated, about 60 to 90 founder minutes a week) [A]**

1. Agents log decisions, numbers and experiment outcomes to the project record.
2. Weekly, an AI drafts scripts from `decisions.md`, `experiments.md` and `budget.md`.
3. AI voice and editing produce the clips, with captions and disclosure labels.
4. A founder approves in one 30-minute block.
5. A scheduler cross-posts.

**Rules**

- Disclose AI voice and AI involvement on every platform.
- Never show customer personal data without consent.
- Show founder names only if the founders choose to.
- Never post invented or rounded-up numbers.
- Every clip ends with one call to action: the newsletter, or the offer once live.
- Track with UTMs. The KPI is newsletter signups and offer clicks, not views.

**Kill or adjust (review at 15 Feb 2027) [A]**

- Below 500 median views after 30 TikTok posts: change the format.
- No newsletter signups from YouTube after 3 episodes: change the hook.
- Documentary production above 90 founder minutes a week: cut to YouTube and TikTok only.
